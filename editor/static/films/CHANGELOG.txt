Film looks for the GR III / GR IIIx Look Editor
================================================

spektrafilm.json in this folder is derived from spektrafilm by Andrea Volpato
(https://github.com/andreavolpato/spektrafilm), whose profiles and LUTs are
licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 (LICENSE.txt, unchanged). The derived file is licensed
CC BY-SA 4.0 as well. The editor's code is not part of it (MIT).

Modified by the GR III Look Editor project
(https://github.com/judgem00/gr3-look-editor), 2026-10-15:

- Rendered with spektrafilm 0.3.4 (commit 3bb2c2d 2026-06-07) in its per-pixel mode
  (no grain, halation or other spatial effects): every negative film on
  every print medium (papers and cine print films), every slide film scanned
  directly; 132 looks. Input scene-linear sRGB, output sRGB.
- Normalised like a scan: the print exposure (negatives) or the film
  exposure (slides) puts 18 % grey at sRGB 0.46, and one scale for all
  channels takes white to sRGB 0.98. Black and the medium's tint stay.
- Sampled as 33^3 LUTs (tools/spektrafilm-lut.py --all) and fitted to the
  GR III's look parameters -- look matrix, RGB curves, HSL and a 3x3 after
  the tone curve -- by core/src/lutfit.rs (core/examples/film_library.rs).
  The file holds those fitted parameters and the fit's error (ΔE2000)
  against the LUT, not the LUTs. The camera reproduces the film only
  approximately (on average 1.9 ΔE2000).

2026-10-16:

- spektrafilm-hc.json: the same 132 looks fitted for the camera's
  highlight correction (core/examples/film_library.rs --hc). The camera
  model reads the sensor out one stop lower and halves the tone curve's
  gain, as measured on a GR III; the fit's samples reach a stop further
  into the highlights. On average 2.1 ΔE2000 from the film (the extra
  highlights are harder to match).
